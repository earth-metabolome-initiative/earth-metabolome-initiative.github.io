## The chemistry of life is still largely unmapped

Every organism produces, transforms, and responds to a vast diversity of
molecules. These metabolites mediate communication, defence, adaptation, and
ecological interactions, yet most of this chemical diversity remains
undocumented or disconnected from the biological context in which it occurs.

The Earth Metabolome Initiative brings together researchers, collections,
laboratories, data specialists, and communities to build a shared view of this
hidden dimension of biodiversity.

## Our vision

We envision an open, global resource that connects chemical profiles with
organisms, environments, specimens, and the knowledge needed to interpret
them. The resource should be useful across metabolomics, ecology, taxonomy,
conservation, natural-product discovery, and education.

## Our goals

1. **Document chemodiversity at global scale.** Develop coordinated approaches
   for sampling biological and environmental diversity.
2. **Connect chemistry to context.** Link metabolomics data to trustworthy
   taxonomic, geographic, ecological, and specimen information.
3. **Build open infrastructure.** Create reusable standards, software, and data
   services that support FAIR and reproducible research.
4. **Grow a collaborative network.** Connect expertise across disciplines,
   institutions, and regions while lowering barriers to participation.
5. **Support discovery and conservation.** Make chemical knowledge available
   for fundamental research and for understanding and protecting biodiversity.

## From DBGI to EMI

The [Digital Botanical Gardens Initiative](https://www.dbgi.org/) is a pilot of
EMI. It develops practical workflows for coordinated sampling, chemical
profiling, metadata capture, and open data integration within botanical garden
collections. EMI extends this ambition beyond gardens and plants toward wild
populations, ecosystems, and the broader diversity of life.
